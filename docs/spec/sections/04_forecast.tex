\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mean against median.} \nBenchBeatXGB{} of the \nBench{} benchmarks have a lower mean WIS than XGBoost, and the best mean score is the \bestMeanLabel{}. On the median the best is \bestMedianLabel{} (XGBoost \mwXGB{} cases, the benchmarks \mwBenchRange{}, the ensemble \mwEns{}). Split at the 90th percentile of weekly cases (\splitThr{}): below it the benchmarks' mean WIS is \splitLoRange{} times XGBoost's, above it \splitHiRange{} times\ifBenchWinHigh, so the benchmarks' better means come from the large-count epidemic weeks\fi. The ensemble's ratio is \ensSplitLo{} below and \ensSplitHi{} above\ifEnsGainsBoth: it gains in both\fi.
\item \textbf{Chronos-2} as first specified, on $\log(1 + \text{incidence})$, \ifChronosLogCollapsed collapsed on one-week reporting spikes\else scored\fi{} (relative WIS \chronosLogRelWIS{}). The headline run uses the incidence scale with past-only covariates; it was chosen \emph{after} seeing the first result. Without covariates it scores \chronosNoCovRelWIS{}, so the covariates \ifChronosCovHelps help\else do not help\fi.
\item \textbf{TabPFN-TS} runs univariate (it ignores past-only covariates) with the ungated TabPFN v2 regressor; the default v3.5 weights need a login and licence and were not used. A lagged-covariate variant scores \tabpfnLagCovRelWIS{}.
\item \textbf{MPNN-LSTM} uses border adjacency (\mpnnBorderEdges{} edges) plus ferry routes (\mpnnFerryEdges{} more, a 2024--2026 proxy); with no graph it scores \mpnnNoneRelWIS{}\ifMpnnGraphSame, the same within seed noise, in line with the sparse lead-lag graph\fi.
\item \textbf{Where it is used.} The benchmarks and the ensemble exist only at the rolling-origin weeks of 2018--2019. Storm scenarios, shifted tracks, 2020 and the storm catalogue use XGBoost with calibrated $R$, because the zero-shot models take no storm-track input.
\end{itemize}
