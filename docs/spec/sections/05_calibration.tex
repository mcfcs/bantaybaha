\section{Surge probability: calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

The model's $R$ was \textbf{under-confident in the middle}: in the reliability bins between \gapBinLo{} and \gapBinHi{}, outcomes came true up to \gapRaw{} more often than forecast (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}). Isotonic regression maps raw $R$ to the observed surge frequency. It is fitted at each origin on earlier forecasts whose four-week outcome was already known (target week no later than the origin), so it never sees the future. It needs \calMinRows{} past outcomes first: the \nUncalOrigins{} origins before \firstCalOrigin{} keep raw $R$ (\nUncalRows{} rows). After recalibration the largest middle-bin gap is \gapCal{}.

Table~\ref{tab:calib} gives the Brier scores. The base rate is \baseRatePct{}, not \surgeExpected{}, because 2019 was an epidemic year and the thresholds come from earlier, quieter years. The scenario files keep both \code{R} (calibrated, used for $P$ and the tiers) and \code{R\_raw}. Recalibration raises mid-range $R$, so more provinces reach the provisional tiers (pre-position at $R \geq \tierPreR$, deploy at $R \geq \tierDeployR$ with $A \geq \tierDeployA$): for Ompong the mean $R$ rises from \ompongRRaw{} to \ompongR{}. The tier thresholds still need review with users.

\textbf{Ties.} Isotonic regression is a step function, so provinces share values: for Ompong the \nUnits{} units take \nDistinctR{} distinct values of $R$ and \nTies{} share the top value (\tieValue{}). A ranking by $R$ alone has ties; $P = R \times A$ breaks them by access, and the backtest's Spearman correlations use average ranks.
