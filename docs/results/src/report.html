Summary

What we built,
what the data says

Bantay-Baha joins three graphs into one ranking: a flood-exposure layer (rain, river routing, observed and mapped flood), a lead-lag outbreak graph learned with PCMCI+, and a road-plus-ferry access graph that a typhoon cuts. A quantile forecast of dengue gives the probability R that a province's next four weeks exceed its usual bad month; two access methods give the share A of people cut off from a hospital; priority is P = R × A. Every number below was computed by build.bat from the raw files.

Findings

  • The forecast beats the bar. Multi-quantile XGBoost reaches relative WIS 0.42 at one week and 0.66 at four over 34,050 rolling-origin forecasts in 2018-2019.
  • Benchmarked, and beaten on the mean. Zero-shot Chronos-2 and TabPFN-TS and a graph network (MPNN-LSTM) reach relative WIS 0.494-0.504, all below XGBoost; their median ensemble with XGBoost is best (0.444, 90% coverage 90%). On the median WIS XGBoost is best (6.23 vs 6.54-6.83): the benchmarks win on high-count epidemic weeks. Storm scenarios still use XGBoost, the only model that takes the storm track.
  • Two honest null results. Lead-lag graph features do not help (graph / no-graph 0.999), nor do rain, river, storm and flood features (1.003). At 1-4 weeks, recent cases and the season carry the skill.
  • Surge probability R, recalibrated. Isotonic regression on past rolling-origin forecasts only lowers the Brier score from 0.089 to 0.082 (base rate 0.216) and removes the model's under-confidence in the middle range.
  • The ranking points at the right places. ρ(R, next-4-week incidence) = 0.68 (Ompong), 0.73 (Tisoy); 7 of Ompong's top-10 provinces surged against a 40% base rate.
  • Access loss, two methods. For the observed tracks (roads cut by Sentinel-1 observed flood) 0.3 M-1.0 M people lose their route on the road-and-ferry network and 0.6 M-1.6 M on the MAP friction raster; the gap is reported as a range. Gale swaths cancel 42-100 of 108 RoRo routes; Kendall's τ under nine setting changes is at least 0.99.
  • Leptospirosis (stretch). In 12 cities, rain is followed by leptospirosis 2-5 weeks later (peak IRR 1.40 per +50 mm/week at lag 4); not extrapolated to provinces.
Not in these results: System Usability Scale responses from target users (the test is built into the dashboard); the DOH facility registry (hospitals are the OSM + NOAH fallback). Scorecard: page 17.

Panel

Step 0: one unit-week table

Harmonised units

The DOH file has 126 reporting units, not 81 provinces: provinces mixed with highly urbanised and component cities, plus misspellings (NUEVA VISCAYA, SISQUIJOR, BASILIG CITY = Bislig). COD-AB uses current boundaries (Maguindanao split in 2022, Special Geographic Area in 2024). The reviewed crosswalk in data/crosswalks/ folds cities into their province and undoes the post-2020 changes, giving 85 units; every dengue unit resolves and every unit has data. A unit-week counts only if every member reported (NaN otherwise, never 0). Deaths are not used (2016 case fatality 3.9%, far above other years), and 2020 fell to 91k cases against 442k in 2019 under COVID-19, so 2020 is never a forecast target.

Population

The 2020 census table's municipal codes are old 9-digit PSGC and do not join to COD-AB for the highly urbanised cities, post-split Maguindanao and the Special Geographic Area. Its own Province column already places cities in their province, so units are summed on it: 108.67 M people.

Layer 1 inputs

Rain: CHIRPS and IMERG agree (weekly r = 0.87). River routing accumulates CHIRPS rain down the HydroRIVERS network. NOAH 25-yr hazard covers 74 of 85 units; the median unit has 20% of its people in a hazard zone. Units with no map are missing, not zero: Basilan, Batanes, Biliran, Camiguin, Davao Occidental, Guimaras, Northern Samar, Romblon, Siquijor, Sulu, Tawi-Tawi.

Storm exposure: every Western Pacific track 2016-2020 from IBTrACS, gale radius from USA R34, else JMA R30, else a fixed 150 km. USA R34 is missing on 29% of Rolly's gale-strength fixes (JMA fills them); the fixed fallback never triggers for the four storms.

Methods

Methods and evaluation protocol

Layer 2: lead-lag outbreak graph

log(1 + incidence), 52-week seasonal difference, standardised inside the training window. PCMCI+ (Runge, 2020) with partial-correlation tests, lags 1-3 weeks, pc_alpha 0.01; lagged inter-province edges are kept when the Benjamini-Hochberg (1995) q-value is below 0.05 (on pure-noise checks PCMCI+ without this step returned many false edges). PageRank (Page et al., 1999) on the reversed graph scores sentinels; Louvain (Blondel et al., 2008) finds communities; lagged cross-correlation with the same FDR is the baseline graph.

Forecast and surge probability

One pooled XGBoost (Chen & Guestrin, 2016) per horizon (1-4 weeks), multi-quantile objective at the 23 CDC FluSight levels, on log(1 + incidence), back-transformed to cases. Features: lagged incidence, same week last year, week of year, CHIRPS / IMERG / river rain lags, storm exposure, NOAH hazard, Sentinel-1 flood and leader signals from the graph; SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017) gives the "drivers". A direct 4-week model gives R, the probability of exceeding the unit's 90th percentile of 4-week incidence in the training window. R is then recalibrated by isotonic regression (Zadrozny & Elkan, 2002) fitted only on earlier rolling-origin forecasts whose outcome was known at the origin.

Benchmarks, scored on exactly the same rows: Chronos-2 (Ansari et al., 2025) and TabPFN-TS (Hoo et al., 2025) zero-shot, each origin's forecast from that unit's own past only; an MPNN-LSTM graph network (Panagopoulos et al., 2021) trained on the same 13-week rolling refits; and the element-wise median of the 23 quantiles of XGBoost and the three (the ensemble).

Evaluation

Rolling origin, never a random split. From January 2018 to December 2019 the model, graph and thresholds are refit every 13 weeks on data up to the refit date, then forecast every origin until the next refit. Scores: weighted interval score on case counts (Bracher et al., 2021), relative WIS against a seasonal-naive forecast, 50% and 90% coverage, and an online conformal 90% band from past errors only (Gibbs & Candès, 2021).

Layer 3: access, two methods

Network. OSM highways become a graph of junctions; RoRo routes are ferry edges (crossing time plus half the headway); hospitals are HOT OSM plus NOAH name-matched facilities (2,199). WorldPop (Tatem, 2017) is summed to 1 km cells, each joined to the nearest road node; a multi-source Dijkstra gives hours to the nearest hospital. Raster. Cost-distance from the same hospitals over the MAP motorised friction surface (Weiss et al., 2020), sea impassable except along the same ferry lines. Storms apply the same rules to both: ferries crossing the gale swath are cancelled; roads (or cells) in observed flood (Sentinel-1, observed tracks) or in NOAH high hazard inside the swath (shifted tracks) are cut, medium hazard slows them. A cell is cut off if it loses its route, or its trip at least doubles and grows by over an hour (after Petricola et al., 2022). The network gives A, P and the tier; the per-unit minimum and maximum of the two methods give A_lo-A_hi and P_lo-P_hi. Under-5 and 60+ people come from Meta HRSL (Tiecke et al., 2017) summed to the same cells; a cell's vulnerable people are cut off when the cell is (network rule).

Reading the comparison

  • Mean vs median. Every benchmark beats XGBoost on mean WIS, and the ensemble is best. On the median row XGBoost is best (6.23 cases vs 6.54-6.83; ensemble 6.29). Split at the 90th percentile of weekly cases (198): below it the benchmarks' mean WIS is 1.02-1.08 times XGBoost's, above it 0.80-0.81 times. The better means come from the large-count epidemic weeks; the ensemble (0.94 / 0.71) gains in both.
  • Chronos-2 as first specified, on log(1 + incidence), collapsed on one-week reporting spikes (relative WIS 112). The headline run uses the incidence scale with past-only covariates; it was chosen after seeing that failure. Without covariates it scores 0.480, so the covariates do not help.
  • TabPFN-TS runs univariate (it ignores past-only covariates) with the ungated TabPFN v2 regressor; the default v3.5 weights need a login and licence and were not used. A lagged-covariate variant scores 0.555.
  • MPNN-LSTM uses border adjacency (133 edges) plus RoRo routes (36 more, a 2024-26 proxy); with no graph it scores 0.492, the same within seed noise, in line with the sparse lead-lag graph.
  • Where it is used. Benchmarks and the ensemble exist only at the rolling-origin weeks (2018-2019). Storm scenarios, shifted tracks, 2020 and the catalogue use XGBoost with calibrated R, because the zero-shot models take no storm-track input.

Calibration

Surge probability R: recalibrated

The model's R was under-confident in the middle: in the bins between 0.1 and 0.7, outcomes came true up to 21% more often than forecast. Isotonic regression maps raw R to observed surge frequency. It is fitted at each origin on earlier forecasts whose 4-week outcome was already known (target week no later than the origin), so it never sees the future. It needs 850 past outcomes first: the 13 origins before 2018-04-08 keep raw R (1,105 rows).

After recalibration the largest middle-bin gap is 7%.

The base rate is 32%, not 10%, because 2019 was an epidemic year and the thresholds come from earlier, quieter years. The scenario files keep both: R (calibrated, used for P and the tiers) and R_raw. Recalibration raises mid-range R, so more provinces reach the provisional tiers (pre-position at R ≥ 0.3, deploy at R ≥ 0.5 with A ≥ 10%): for Ompong mean R rises from 0.22 to 0.29. The tier thresholds still need review with users.

Ties. Isotonic regression is a step function, so provinces share values: for Ompong the 85 units take 18 distinct R values and 4 share the top value (0.99). A ranking by R alone has ties; P = R × A breaks them by access, and the backtest's Spearman correlations use average ranks.

Graph

The lead-lag graph

Final window: 84 series, T = 156 weeks after seasonal differencing (Davao Occidental sits out with more than 10% missing weeks). PCMCI+ keeps 8 lagged inter-province links at q < 0.05. Plain lagged cross-correlation with the same FDR keeps 4,265, because the 2019 national epidemic makes almost every pair co-move.

The proposal's sentinel idea is therefore weakly supported at province-week level. The links are statistical dependencies, not evidence of transmission, and several join distant provinces. Likely reasons: weekly province totals average out local waves, four years is short for 84 series, and reporting artefacts dominate small provinces. Synchrony studies such as van Panhuis et al. (2015) worked at longer time scales. The MPNN-LSTM benchmark points the same way: its result does not depend on its graph.

Flood

Observed flood: Sentinel-1

Radar sees through typhoon clouds. For each typhoon, new open water is mapped on Google Earth Engine (Gorelick et al., 2017) from Sentinel-1 VH backscatter in 100 m tiles near the track: before = median of days -30 to -3, after = darkest image of days 0 to +10, both from the same relative orbit. A pixel is new flood when VH drops by at least 3 dB and ends at most -18 dB; permanent water (JRC), slopes over 5° and specks under 8 pixels are removed (after UN-SPIDER, n.d.). The first version, UN-SPIDER's after/before ratio on dB values with mixed orbits, recovered almost none of the EMS flood and was replaced.

Two methods

Network vs friction raster

The proposal runs a raster method next to the network because OSM completeness is uneven, and reports their disagreement as uncertainty. Both use the same hospitals, population cells, ferry lines and storm rules.

On a normal day

The raster reaches more people (99.5% vs 97.3%) and is faster (population-weighted mean 0.19 h vs 0.24 h; median cell ratio raster / network 0.62): it can cross land without a mapped road. The two still rank places alike: Spearman 0.89 over 321,701 cells that both reach, 0.95 over the 85 units' mean trip.

Under a storm

The gap changes sign with the flood source. With Sentinel-1 the raster cuts off more people (0.6 M-1.6 M vs 0.3 M-1.0 M); with the NOAH proxy it cuts off fewer (median ratio 0.66). A likely reason: at 1 km a flooded cell centre closes the whole cell, while radar flood is patchy and the network closes only the flooded road edges; the proxy closes every high-hazard road, which the raster's cell-centre test samples more sparsely. The unit rankings agree well under the proxy (ρ of A 0.91-0.99) and less with radar (0.78-0.86), where 6-7 of the top-10 provinces by P are shared. The dashboard shows A and P with this range. Sensitivity on the raster: Kendall's τ ≥ 0.999 for the same nine variants.

Vulnerable groups

Meta HRSL puts 11.1% of people under five and 6.7% aged 60+; the 2020 census has 10.2% and 8.5%. HRSL therefore under-counts older people (and slightly over-counts young children), so the 60+ cut-off numbers are low estimates. Among those cut off, the under-5 share is 7.9%-9.5% and the 60+ share 5.2%-6.2%.

Leptospirosis

Leptospirosis: the stretch goal

The proposal assumed no open subnational leptospirosis series. Project CCHAIN (HDX) has weekly PIDSR counts for 12 cities, and the Mendeley (Parreño) file has national weeks. Both are analysed as retrospective associations, not forecasts: negative-binomial models of weekly cases on CHIRPS rain 0-6 weeks earlier, with seasonal terms, year and city effects and a COVID-19 term.

  • Cities (3,036 city-weeks, 1,685 cases, 2016-2020): the headline. Rain is followed by leptospirosis 2-5 weeks later; the joint peak is lag 4, IRR 1.40 (1.29-1.52) per +50 mm/week, cumulative over lags 0-6 2.45 (2.05-2.92). A heavy-rain week (at or above the city's 90th percentile) peaks at lag 4: 2.00 (1.61-2.48). That is a week or two later than the ~2-week peak of Matsushita et al. (2018); the CCHAIN week label is uncertain by about a week.
  • National (80 weeks, 2019-2020): consistent, weaker. Joint peak at lag 5: 1.68 (1.28-2.22); cumulative 4.12 (2.41-7.03). With 2019 alone the cumulative effect is 1.51 (0.76-3.00): much of the national signal comes from 2020 (the December surge after Rolly and Ulysses, under COVID reporting).
  • Typhoons: mixed. After Rolly and Ulysses (2020), cities inside the gale swath had 4.31 (1.72-10.85) times the outside cities' observed / expected cases in weeks 1-4 (pre-landfall placebo 1.20, 0.32-4.53). For Ompong and Tisoy the ratio is 0.81 (0.55-1.20) but the placebo fails (2.36, 1.55-3.61): weeks of monsoon rain had already raised cases before Ompong. Four storms and 12 cities support no general storm effect.
  • Flood: not estimable. Of 48 city-storm pairs, 31 had radar over more than 20% of the city and 8 show any new flood (at most 2.2%); too few to relate flood to cases. Flood mediation stays a literature assumption.

The 12 cities are not a sample of provinces, so nothing here is extrapolated to provinces, and leptospirosis is not in the dashboard's ranking.

Proposal corrections found in the data

  • 126 dengue reporting units, not 81 provinces (harmonised to 85); deaths unreliable; 2020 collapsed under COVID-19.
  • An open subnational leptospirosis series exists (Project CCHAIN, 12 cities).
  • EMSR478 (Rolly) has damage grading only, no flood delineation; the Global Flood Database (Tellman et al., 2021) has no Philippine event after 2017.
  • Flood hazard maps come from the UP NOAH archive at 5 and 25 years, not LiPAD; 11 units have no 25-yr map.
  • HydroRIVERS (Lehner & Grill, 2013) places the Philippines in the Australasia file, not Asia.
  • roroph routes describe 2024-2026 operations with partly coarse port coordinates; NHFR blocks scripted download (OSM + NOAH fallback used).
  • Meta HRSL under-counts people aged 60+ against the 2020 census.
  • The proposal's final forecast was the median ensemble; storm scenarios use XGBoost (the only storm-conditioned model).

Limits

Limitations and next steps

Threats to validity

  • Two evaluation years. 2018-2019 contains one epidemic; the model ranking changes between mean and median WIS because of it.
  • Surveillance artefacts. Reporting delays and under-reporting are in the target; forecasts predict reported, not true, cases.
  • Observed flood. Sentinel-1 sees each place every 6-12 days and misses flooding under dense vegetation and in built-up areas; shifted tracks and the catalogue fall back to static hazard maps.
  • Network gaps. OSM completeness is uneven outside Luzon (the raster method bounds this); ferries are 2024-2026 routes; hospitals are an OSM / NOAH fallback without facility level or capacity. HRSL under-counts older people.
  • Provisional rules. Tier thresholds, road speeds and the cut-off rule are assumptions, partly addressed by the sensitivity analysis.
  • Causality. PCMCI+ links and the leptospirosis lags are associations, not transmission routes or causal effects.

Next steps

  1. Run the System Usability Scale test with three DOH / LGU / DRRM users (Brooke, 1996) and review the tiers with them.
  2. Feed a PAGASA forecast track and its cone in place of historical shifts.
  3. Add a second radar or optical source between Sentinel-1 passes.
  4. Replace the hospital fallback with the DOH facility registry (facility level, so access can mean a Level 2-3 hospital).
  5. Give the benchmarks storm inputs, or blend the ensemble with XGBoost's storm signal, so scenarios can use the ensemble.

Reproducibility

setup.bat (or download_data.bat) → build.bat → run_app.bat. This report and the slides are regenerated with python scripts/make_results.py. Missing inputs are skipped, listed in data/processed/status.json and on the dashboard; re-running after they arrive needs no code change.
