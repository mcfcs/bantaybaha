Summary

What we built,
what the data says

Bantay-Baha joins three graphs into one ranking: a flood-exposure layer (rain, river routing, flood hazard), a lead-lag outbreak graph learned with PCMCI+, and a road-plus-ferry access graph that a typhoon cuts. A quantile forecast of dengue gives the probability R that a province's next four weeks exceed its usual bad month; the access model gives the share A of people cut off from a hospital; priority is P = R × A. Every number below was computed by build.bat from the raw files; nothing is synthetic.

Findings

  • The forecast beats the bar. Pooled multi-quantile XGBoost reaches relative WIS 0.42 at one week and 0.66 at four weeks over 34,170 rolling-origin forecasts in 2018-2019; 90% intervals cover 85%.
  • Two honest null results. Lead-lag graph features do not improve the forecast (relative WIS graph / no-graph = 0.999), and adding rain, river and flood-hazard features moved relative WIS only from 0.547 to 0.545. At 1-4 weeks, recent cases and the season carry the skill.
  • Surge probability is informative but under-confident. Brier 0.089 vs 0.216 for the base rate; forecasts of 0.1-0.7 come true 10-20 points more often. It needs recalibration before the tier thresholds are trusted.
  • The ranking points at the right places. Spearman(R, observed next-4-week incidence) = 0.69 for Ompong and 0.74 for Tisoy; 7 of Ompong's top-10 provinces by priority surged against a 40% base rate.
  • Access loss is large and robust to settings. The typhoons' gale swaths cancel 42-100 of 108 RoRo routes and cut off 0.3 M-1.0 M people (roads cut by Sentinel-1 observed flood); Kendall's τ of the ranking under nine setting changes is at least 0.99.
Not yet in these results: the MAP friction raster access method (data on disk, not wired into the scenarios); the DOH NHFR hospital list and PSA PSGC (manual downloads); the Chronos-2 / TabPFN-TS benchmarks. See the scorecard (page 13).

Panel

Step 0: one unit-week table

Harmonised units

The DOH file has 126 reporting units, not 81 provinces: provinces mixed with 45 highly urbanised and component cities, plus misspellings (NUEVA VISCAYA, SISQUIJOR, BASILIG CITY = Bislig). COD-AB uses current boundaries (Maguindanao split in 2022, Special Geographic Area in 2024). The reviewed crosswalk in data/crosswalks/ folds cities into their province and undoes the post-2020 changes, giving 85 units; all 126 dengue units resolve and every unit has data. A unit-week counts only if every member reported (NaN otherwise, never 0).

Population

The 2020 census table's municipal codes are old 9-digit PSGC and do not join to COD-AB for 54 municipalities (24.2 M people). Its own Province column already places cities in their province, so units are summed on it: 108.67 M people.

Layer 1 inputs

Rain: CHIRPS and IMERG agree (weekly r = 0.87). River routing accumulates CHIRPS rain down the HydroRIVERS network. NOAH 25-yr hazard covers 74 of 85 units; the median unit has 20% of its people in a hazard zone. Units with no map are missing, not zero: Basilan, Batanes, Biliran, Camiguin, Davao Occidental, Guimaras, Northern Samar, Romblon, Siquijor, Sulu, Tawi-Tawi.

Storm exposure: every Western Pacific track 2016-2020 from IBTrACS, gale radius from USA R34, else JMA R30, else a fixed 150 km (the fallback never triggered for the four backtest storms).

Methods

Methods and evaluation protocol

Layer 2: lead-lag outbreak graph

log(1 + incidence), 52-week seasonal difference, standardised inside the training window. PCMCI+ (Runge, 2020) with partial-correlation tests, lags 1-3 weeks, pc_alpha 0.01; lagged inter-province edges are kept when the Benjamini-Hochberg (1995) q-value is below 0.05. The FDR step matters: on pure noise with 85 series, PCMCI+ alone returned 60-90 false edges. PageRank (Page et al., 1999) on the reversed graph scores sentinels; Louvain (Blondel et al., 2008) finds communities; lagged cross-correlation with the same FDR is the baseline graph.

Forecast

One pooled XGBoost (Chen & Guestrin, 2016) per horizon (1-4 weeks) with the multi-quantile objective at the 23 CDC FluSight levels, on log(1 + incidence), back-transformed to cases. Features: lagged incidence and moving averages, same week last year, week of year, CHIRPS / IMERG / river rain lags, storm exposure, NOAH hazard, and leader signals from the graph. SHAP values (Lundberg & Lee, 2017) from the median model give the dashboard's "drivers". A second model predicts the 4-week cumulative incidence directly; R is its probability of exceeding the unit's 90th percentile of 4-week incidence in the training window.

Evaluation

Rolling origin, never a random split. From January 2018 to December 2019 the model, the lead-lag graph and the surge thresholds are refit every 13 weeks on data up to the refit date only, then forecast every origin until the next refit. Training targets never pass the refit date and 2020 is never a target. Scores: weighted interval score on case counts (Bracher et al., 2021), relative WIS against a seasonal-naive forecast with empirical error quantiles, 50% and 90% coverage, and an online conformal 90% band built from past errors only (Gibbs & Candès, 2021) as a check on the quantiles.

Layer 3: access

OSM highways (motorway to track) become a graph of junctions; RoRo routes are ferry edges (crossing time plus half the headway); hospitals are HOT OSM plus NOAH name-matched facilities (2,199) until NHFR arrives. WorldPop (Tatem, 2017) is summed to 1 km cells, each joined to the nearest road node. A multi-source Dijkstra gives hours to the nearest hospital. A storm cancels ferry routes crossing its gale swath and cuts roads in observed flood (Sentinel-1, observed tracks) or, for shifted tracks, cuts (high hazard) or slows (medium) roads in NOAH 25-yr hazard inside the swath. A cell is cut off if it loses its route, or its trip at least doubles and grows by more than an hour (after Petricola et al., 2022).

Ablations and calibration

The graph ablation follows from the graph itself: with so few significant links, leader signals are empty for most provinces. The rain result has two likely reasons, not yet tested: rain moves with the season in a four-year panel, so the season features already carry it, and any effect of rain at lags longer than four weeks is outside this forecast horizon. Both are reported as findings rather than tuned away.

Surge probability R

Brier score 0.089 against 0.216 for always predicting the base rate. The base rate is 32%, not 10%, because 2019 was an epidemic year and the thresholds come from earlier, quieter years.

R is under-confident in the middle range: forecasts of 0.1-0.7 come true 10-20 points more often. Isotonic recalibration fitted on past rolling-origin forecasts only is the next step, before the tier thresholds (R ≥ 0.3, R ≥ 0.5) are relied on.

Graph

The lead-lag graph

Final window: 84 series, T = 156 weeks after seasonal differencing (Davao Occidental sits out with more than 10% missing weeks). PCMCI+ keeps 8 lagged inter-province links at q < 0.05. Plain lagged cross-correlation with the same FDR keeps 4,265, because the 2019 national epidemic makes almost every pair co-move.

The proposal's sentinel idea is therefore weakly supported at province-week level. The links are statistical dependencies, not evidence of transmission, and several join distant provinces. Likely reasons: weekly province totals average out local waves, four years is short for 84 series, and reporting artefacts dominate small provinces. Synchrony studies such as van Panhuis et al. (2015) worked at longer time scales.

Flood

Observed flood: Sentinel-1

Radar sees through typhoon clouds. For each typhoon, new open water is mapped on Google Earth Engine (Gorelick et al., 2017) by comparing Sentinel-1 VH backscatter before and after landfall, in 100 m tiles near the track: before = median of days -30 to -3, after = darkest image of days 0 to +10, both from the same relative orbit, so the viewing geometry matches. A pixel is new flood when VH drops by at least 3 dB and ends water-dark (at most -18 dB); permanent water (JRC), slopes over 5° and specks under 8 pixels are removed (after UN-SPIDER, n.d.).

Checked against Copernicus EMS

Inside the EMS areas of interest, EMS mapped 108 km² of observed flood and Sentinel-1 flagged 46 km²; 26 km² overlap. So 55% of Sentinel-1 flood there is confirmed by EMS, and Sentinel-1 recovers 24% of the EMS area.

The first version followed UN-SPIDER's after/before ratio on dB values and recovered almost none of the EMS flood: dividing negative dB values under-detects surfaces that were already dark, and mixing orbits compared different viewing geometries. Sampling backscatter at EMS flood points showed drops of 4-10 dB that the ratio missed. Low recall is expected: EMS used other imagery and dates, while one Sentinel-1 orbit passed here only on 16 and 22 September.

Priority

Priority: Ompong 2018

Ompong made landfall in Cagayan on 15 September 2018. The model, trained on data to the landfall week only, puts Pampanga first, with 0 provinces in the "deploy mobile clinics" tier and 21 in "pre-position supplies". Provinces with high R but little observed loss of access fall to "pre-position"; "deploy" needs at least 10% of people cut off, which observed flood alone did not produce for this storm (the hazard-map upper bound did, for 7 provinces).

The dashboard repeats this for every storm and 14 shifted variants of each track (±0.5° and ±1° north, ±1° east), so a health officer can ask "what if it comes in further south?" and get an answer in seconds.

Metro Manila benchmark

Cutting every NCR road in NOAH 25-yr medium or high hazard leaves 29.7% of NCR without a route within the cut-off rule. Tariverdi et al. (2023) report about 22% losing access to higher-level care at a 15 cm flood. The definitions differ, so this is an order-of-magnitude check, not a replication.

Proposal corrections found in the data

  • 126 dengue reporting units, not 81 provinces; deaths unreliable (2016 case fatality 3.9%); 2020 collapsed under COVID-19.
  • An open subnational leptospirosis series exists (Project CCHAIN, 12 cities, 2008-2022).
  • EMSR478 (Rolly) has damage grading only, no flood delineation; the Global Flood Database (Tellman et al., 2021) ends in 2018.
  • Flood hazard maps come from the UP NOAH archive, not LiPAD; 11 units have no 25-yr map.
  • HydroRIVERS (Lehner & Grill, 2013) places the Philippines in the Australasia file, not Asia.
  • IBTrACS (Knapp et al., 2010) USA R34 is missing on up to 58% of fixes of the backtest storms; JMA R30 fills the gaps.
  • roroph routes describe 2024-2026 operations with partly coarse port coordinates.

Limits

Limitations and next steps

Threats to validity

  • Two evaluation years. 2018-2019 contains one epidemic; skill and calibration may differ in quieter or COVID-era years.
  • Surveillance artefacts. Reporting delays and under-reporting are in the target; forecasts predict reported, not true, cases.
  • Observed flood. Sentinel-1 sees each place every 6-12 days and misses flooding under dense vegetation and in built-up areas; shifted tracks fall back to static hazard maps.
  • Network gaps. OSM completeness is uneven outside Luzon; ferries are 2024-2026 routes; hospitals are an OSM / NOAH fallback without facility level or capacity.
  • Provisional rules. Action-tier thresholds, road speeds and the cut-off rule are assumptions, partly addressed by the sensitivity analysis.
  • Causality. PCMCI+ links are conditional dependencies, not transmission routes.

Next steps

  1. Add a second radar or optical source between Sentinel-1 passes and compare observed-flood A with the hazard proxy.
  2. Wire in the MAP friction raster method (Weiss et al., 2020) and report the network-vs-raster gap per province.
  3. Recalibrate R and review the tiers with DOH and LGU users; run the System Usability Scale test.
  4. Replace the hospital fallback with NHFR; validate pcodes against PSGC 2Q 2026.
  5. Run the Chronos-2 benchmark (Ansari et al., 2025); extend to leptospirosis with the CCHAIN city series.

Reproducibility

setup.bat (or download_data.bat) → build.bat → run_app.bat. This report and the slides are regenerated with python scripts/make_results.py. Missing inputs are skipped, listed in data/processed/status.json and on the dashboard; re-running after they arrive needs no code change.
