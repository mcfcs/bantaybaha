Signal 02 / ProblemRationale: problem statement

Typhoons bring floods.
Floods bring disease.

8-9

tropical cyclones cross the Philippines every year, mostly from July to October (PAGASA)

2,089

leptospirosis cases and 162 deaths after Typhoon Ondoy in 2009, five times the national lepto deaths of 2008 (Balbuena et al., 2010)

146k

dengue cases from January to July 2019, when DOH declared a national epidemic

Who is hit hardest

Floodwater spreads leptospirosis within days; standing water breeds the mosquitoes behind dengue for weeks after. The same storm closes roads and grounds ferries, so the most exposed communities are often the ones that cannot reach a hospital.

Signal 03 / The cascadeRationale: problem and motivation

The response arrives after the surge

A typhoon sets off a sequence. Each step is predictable from data that already exist, yet today's response starts only at the last one.

DAY 0

Landfall

Gale-force winds; ferries suspended; roads blocked by landslides and debris

DAYS 1-7

Flooding

Rivers carry upstream rain to the lowlands; towns at river mouths flood after the rain stops

WEEKS 1-2

Leptospirosis

Risk from rainfall peaks about two weeks later and runs through flooding (Matsushita et al., 2018)

WEEKS 2-8

Dengue

Breeding sites multiply; cases rise province by province

TODAY

Response

Prophylaxis, vector control and medical teams go out after cases rise; isolated places are reached last

What is missing is a tool that looks at this storm and says, before the roads close, where disease will rise and who will be cut off.

Dengue forecasts exist

Climate-driven, province-level (Buczak et al., 2014), but not for a specific storm and without honest ranges

Access studies exist

Flood access loss for one city, Metro Manila (Tariverdi et al., 2023), with no health forecast and no ferries

Outbreak waves are known

Dengue travels between regions (van Panhuis et al., 2015), but this is unused for storm response

Signal 04 / AccessRationale: problem and motivation

An archipelago makes access the problem

  • An island nation. Many provinces reach the nearest hospital only by RoRo ferry; a gale warning cancels the crossing
  • Roads in floodplains. The road to the provincial hospital often runs exactly where the river overflows
  • Measured once, for one city. A 15 cm flood leaves about 22% of Metro Manila without access to higher-level care (Tariverdi et al., 2023); nothing comparable exists nationwide, or with ferries

Guiding question

Can flood exposure, a graph of outbreak spread and a storm-disrupted transport network, combined with calibrated forecasts, rank the provinces that need help first, early enough to act?

Signal 05 / DesignMethodology and approach

Three graphs, one ranking

1 / Flood exposure

Sentinel-1 observed flood, CHIRPS + IMERG rain, rain routed down rivers, UP NOAH hazard, typhoon gale swaths

2 / Lead-lag graph

PCMCI+ finds which provinces rise first; their recent rise feeds every follower's forecast

Quantile forecast

pooled XGBoost, 23 quantiles, 1-4 weeks ahead, tested by rolling origin and scored by WIS

R / surge risk

chance the next 4 weeks beat the province's usual bad month

3 / Access graph

OSM roads + 108 RoRo ferry routes + hospitals; a storm cancels ferries and cuts flooded roads

A / access loss

share of people who lose their route to a hospital

P = R × A

ranked provinces, drivers and an action tier for a real or shifted track

Each half is validated on its own: forecast vs a seasonal baseline; access vs Metro Manila and Copernicus.

Signal 08 / MethodTechnical rigor: validity of techniques

Honest ranges, tested like real time

Model

  • One pooled XGBoost per horizon, 1-4 weeks ahead
  • 23 quantiles (the CDC FluSight standard), so every forecast is a range
  • Inputs: recent cases, season, rain, river, storm, flood, leader provinces
  • A direct 4-week model gives the surge probability R; SHAP explains each one

Evaluation without leakage

  • Rolling origin over 2018-2019: refit every 13 weeks on past data only
  • The outbreak graph and surge thresholds are re-learned inside each window
  • Weighted interval score on case counts (Bracher et al., 2021)
  • Bar to beat: "same week last year" (seasonal naive)

Signal 11 / Result 3Technical rigor: analytical depth

Surge probability R: it ranks provinces well

0.089

Brier score of R, against 0.216 for always predicting the base rate

  • When R says high, surges follow: above 0.7, 95% of province-weeks surged
  • In the middle range R under-states the risk (forecasts of 0.1-0.7 verify 10-20 points higher), so mid-range provinces deserve attention
  • 2019's epidemic made surges common (32% of province-weeks)

Signal 18 / DeployFuture directions: real-world application

Putting it in the loop

1

Storm watch

PAGASA issues a tropical cyclone bulletin. Its forecast track goes in, along with shifted tracks for the cone of uncertainty.

2

Pre-landfall bulletin

Within minutes: a ranked province list with action tiers to DOH regional offices, provincial health offices and DRRMOs, 48-72 h ahead.

3

Post-landfall update

Days 1-7: Sentinel-1 flood maps replace the hazard estimate; ferry and road status updates who is cut off.

4

Learn every week

Weeks 1-8: each PIDSR report is compared with the forecast; the model recalibrates and tiers are adjusted.

The output is an action, not a chart: monitor sentinel sites, pre-position doxycycline and vector control, or send mobile clinics before the roads and ferries close.

DOH regional office

Ranked provinces with action tiers and expected cases (90% range) for the next 4 weeks

Provincial health office

Which municipalities lose hospital access, and the drivers behind the risk

DRRMO

Ferry routes and roads expected to close: where mobile clinics must go first

Built to be used, not just shown

Where the users are

  • Web dashboard for regional epidemiologists
  • One-page PDF bulletin per storm for provincial health offices
  • SMS summary for the "deploy" provinces
  • Low bandwidth: maps need no tile server

Designed with them

  • Action tiers co-designed with DOH and LGU health officers and DRRMOs
  • Filipino and English labels
  • Usability tested (System Usability Scale) before each typhoon season

Runs on what exists

  • Weekly PIDSR counts, PAGASA tracks, Sentinel-1, the DOH facility registry
