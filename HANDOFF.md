## Sentinel-1 (2026-10-24)

- **Method had to change.** UN-SPIDER's after/before ratio > 1.25 on dB values, with mixed orbits, recovered almost none of
  Copernicus EMSR312's observed flood at Tuguegarao (Ompong). Backscatter sampled at EMS flood points showed real drops of
  4-10 dB that the ratio missed (dividing negative dB values under-detects already-dark surfaces). Fixed: same relative orbit
  before/after, a dB-difference rule, a 10-day after window. Against EMS over all its AOIs: **55% of Sentinel-1 flood is
  confirmed by EMS (precision), Sentinel-1 recovers 24% of the EMS area (recall)**. Low recall is expected (one orbit, passes on
  16 and 22 Sep; EMS used other imagery).
- **People in new flood (landfall week):** Ompong 0.5 M (most: Pampanga), Tisoy 1.1 M (Isabela), Rolly 0.6 M (Camarines Sur),
  Ulysses 1.4 M (Cagayan; was 1.5 M when its map was read from panel weeks merged with Rolly).
- **Access loss now uses observed flood for the real tracks** and drops 10-20x: people cut off 0.34 M (Ompong), 0.70 M (Tisoy),
  0.36 M (Rolly), 1.03 M (Ulysses), vs 4.8-10.3 M with the NOAH proxy. Report both: radar = lower bound, proxy = upper bound.
  No province reaches "deploy" (needs A >= 10%); Ompong has 21 at "pre-position" and its top priority moves from Cagayan to
  **Pampanga**. Shifted tracks still use the proxy (no image of a storm that did not happen).
- **R and the forecast barely move** (relative WIS 0.544 after the review fixes below, 90% coverage 0.85; S1 exists only for 4 storm windows). Backtest
  Spearman(R) 0.69 / 0.74; Spearman(P) fell (Tisoy -0.05) because A is now small and noisy. Sensitivity tau still >= 0.99.
- Fixes found on the way: Rolly and Ulysses windows overlap, which duplicated 2 panel weeks (now max per unit-week); the
  per-edge S1 lookup took 44 s x 36 in sensitivity (now vectorised + cached, 0.6 s); the scenario parquet labels the source
  "observed" (dashboard and results map it to "Sentinel-1 observed flood"; the code now writes "sentinel1").

## Results (full data, 2026-10-24)

1. **Crosswalk**: 126/126 dengue units -> 85 harmonised units (82 by ADM2 name, 44 cities via ADM3; NAGA CITY -> Camarines
   Sur). Every unit has dengue data.
2. **Census join**: the HDX 2020 CPH `MunCode` is the old 9-digit PSGC; even through the file's own `muncode lookup` sheet,
   54 municipalities (24.2 M people: HUCs, post-split Maguindanao, SGA) don't match COD-AB ADM3 pcodes. Population is
   summed on the census `Province` column instead (HUCs already sit under their province): 108.67 M in 85 units.
3. **Panel coverage**: rain 99% (CHIRPS vs IMERG weekly r = 0.87), river routing 99%, NOAH 25-yr hazard 74/85 units (no
   map: Basilan, Batanes, Biliran, Camiguin, Davao Occidental, Guimaras, Northern Samar, Romblon, Siquijor, Sulu,
   Tawi-Tawi; carried as NaN), 5-yr 82%. Median unit share of population in any 25-yr hazard class: 20%.
4. **Lead-lag graph is very sparse.** PCMCI+ on 2016-2019 (84 series, T = 156; Davao Occidental sits out with >10% missing
   weeks) keeps **8 inter-province edges** at BH q < 0.05; the rolling windows give 0 edges in every 2018 window and 2-6
   in 2019. On pure-noise checks PCMCI+ without the FDR filter returns 60-90 false edges. Lagged cross-correlation gives
   4,265 edges (the shared 2019 epidemic). **The sentinel idea is weakly supported at province-week level**; present it
   as such.
5. **Forecast skill** (rolling origin 2018-2019, refit every 13 weeks, 34,050 forecasts scored on rows where every model has a
   score, case counts; build of 2026-10-24 after the review fixes):

   | horizon | WIS xgb | WIS seasonal-naive | relative WIS | 50% cov | 90% cov | 90% cov (online conformal) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 wk | 14.7 | 35.1 | **0.42** | 0.47 | 0.89 | 0.88 |
   | 2 wk | 18.1 | 35.4 | **0.51** | 0.44 | 0.86 | 0.87 |
   | 3 wk | 21.0 | 35.6 | **0.59** | 0.42 | 0.84 | 0.86 |
   | 4 wk | 23.6 | 35.9 | **0.66** | 0.40 | 0.82 | 0.85 |
   | all | 19.3 | 35.5 | **0.54** | 0.43 | 0.85 | 0.87 |

   Both proposal targets are met (relative WIS < 1; 90% coverage inside 80-95%, the 4-week horizon close to the floor).
   50% intervals are a little narrow. **Ablations** (both computed in the same build, same rows): graph vs no-graph
   relative WIS = 0.999; with vs without rain / river / storm / flood features = 1.003 (0.544 vs 0.542 against
   seasonal-naive). At 1-4 weeks the skill comes from recent cases and season.
6. **Surge probability R**: Brier 0.089 vs 0.216 for the base rate (32% of unit-weeks surged in 2018-2019, the 2019
   epidemic). R is **under-confident in the middle**: forecasts of 0.1-0.7 verify 10-20 points higher
   (`surge_reliability.csv`). Recalibrate before trusting the tier thresholds.
7. **Access baseline** (network method): 369,121 one-km population cells (109.4 M people), 1.72 M road nodes / 2.13 M
   edges (383,000 km), 108/108 ferry routes snapped, 2,199 fallback hospitals. 97.3% of people have a route; the
   population-weighted mean trip is 0.24 h. A full solve takes ~4 s.
8. **Storm scenarios** (observed tracks; roads cut/slowed by the NOAH 25-yr proxy inside the gale swath, plus the EMS AOIs
   for Ompong):

   | storm | ferry routes cancelled | people cut off | mean A |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Ompong | 42 | 7.5 M | 6.4% |
   | Tisoy | 100 | 10.3 M | 9.2% |
   | Rolly | 42 | 4.8 M | 2.9% |
   | Ulysses | 54 | 7.8 M | 6.9% |

   Ompong: 7 units at "deploy", 18 at "pre-position", top priority Cagayan (its landfall province). The cut-off counts
   come from a proxy (every high-hazard road inside the whole-lifetime gale swath is cut) and are probably upper bounds;
   Sentinel-1 will replace the proxy for the observed tracks.
9. **Backtest** (did high-priority units see more cases in the 4 weeks after?): Spearman(R, observed / threshold) = 0.69
   Ompong, 0.74 Tisoy (0.48 / 0.35 for the 2020 storms, indicative only). Spearman with P is lower (-0.05 to 0.21, A from
   Sentinel-1): A is about access, not cases. Ompong: 7 of the top-10 units by P surged, against a 40% base rate.
10. **Sensitivity**: Kendall's tau of the P ranking is 0.99-1.00 for every variant (speed x0.8/x1.2, ferry rule,
    medium-hazard slow-down, cut-off ratio 1.5/3, gale fallback 100/200 km). The variants do change A (speed +-20% moves
    mean A ~4%), but P's ranking is dominated by R, which spans 0.02-0.93 across units. The fixed gale fallback never
    triggers: every gale-strength fix of the four storms has a USA or JMA radius.
11. **Metro Manila benchmark**: cutting every NCR road in NOAH 25-yr medium/high hazard leaves 29.7% of NCR without a route
    within the cut-off rule, vs ~22% in Tariverdi et al. (2023) for a 15 cm flood (different flood and facility
    definitions: an order-of-magnitude check only).

## Findings so far (checked against the real files)

### Proposal corrections

1. **The dengue file has 126 reporting units, not 81 provinces.** It mixes provinces with 45 HUC/NCR cities (Cebu City,
   Davao City, all NCR cities, …) and has misspellings: `NUEVA VISCAYA`, `SISQUIJOR`, `BASILIG CITY` (= Bislig City, Surigao del Sur: listed under CARAGA,
   so not Basilan), `NORTH COTABATO`,
   and leading spaces in `" CAGAYAN DE ORO CITY"` and `" NEGROS ORIENTAL"`. Row 2 of the CSV holds HXL tags (skip it).
   The data run from 2016-01-10 to 2021-01-10, weekly, on Sundays. Matching names to COD-AB needs care: city units must be
   looked up as cities first (`QUEZON CITY` ≠ Quezon province, `COTABATO CITY` ≠ Cotabato, `SAN JUAN`/`SANTIAGO CITY` ≠ the
   same-name towns), `ñ` must be folded (Las Piñas, Parañaque), and `NAGA CITY` is ambiguous (Camarines Sur vs Cebu; Region V
   says Camarines Sur). With that, 126/126 units resolve (notebook section 5).
2. **Dengue deaths are unreliable.** 2016 has 8,127 deaths (about 3.9% CFR) against 1,195–4,563 in the other years. Don't use deaths.
3. **2020 cases fell to 91k (2019: 442k)** because of the COVID reporting collapse. The proposal's "2020 for access only" holds.
4. **A subnational leptospirosis series is openly available.** Project CCHAIN (HDX) has weekly PIDSR city counts, 2008–2022,
   for leptospirosis, dengue and 7 other diseases across its 12 cities, which spans all four backtest typhoons. The
   proposal says "no open subnational series exists" and plans to digitise DOH reports; revisit that.
5. **EMSR478 (Rolly) has only damage-grading products (4 AOIs), no flood delineation**, so it can't cross-check
   Sentinel-1 flood extent. EMSR312 (Ompong) does have observed-flood delineation, for 6 Northern Luzon AOIs
   (Tuguegarao, Ilagan, Vigan, Candon, Sudipen, Aparri): 48 km² observed flood at first delineation (`MAP`) and 120 km² in the
   later monitoring product (`MONIT01`) of the same AOIs, so don't add the two. No EMS activation was found for Tisoy or Ulysses.
6. **The flood hazard maps come from the UP NOAH Google Drive, not LiPAD.** They are per province at 5/25/100-year return periods, and
   coverage is partial (67 files at 5-year, 72 at 25-year). Matched to COD-AB by shapefile name (or old 9-digit PSGC in 8 files),
   the 25-year maps cover 75/88 ADM2 units (5-year: 71/88). No 25-year map for Basilan, Batanes, Biliran, Camiguin, City of Isabela,
   Davao Occidental, Guimaras, Northern Samar, Romblon, Siquijor, Special Geographic Area, Sulu, Tawi-Tawi, so "share of
   population in a hazard zone" is undefined there (carry as missing). Some zips hold two provinces (Cotabato + South Cotabato,
   Samar + Eastern Samar), and Maguindanao (pre-split), Metro Manila and Mindoro come as one file each.
7. **COD-AB uses current boundaries (88 ADM2 units)**: 4 NCR districts, Maguindanao del Norte/Sur (split in 2022), the
   "Special Geographic Area" (2024) and "City of Isabela". The 2016–2020 dengue data use the old units, so a harmonised
   historical crosswalk is required.
8. **The IBTrACS 34-kt gale radius (USA_R34_NE) is missing on many fixes**: Ompong 85%, Tisoy 71%, Ulysses 69%, Rolly only 42%
   (notebook section 6). The ferry-closure buffer needs a fallback (TOKYO_R30 or a fixed radius).
9. **roroph** (CRAN/r-universe `pinasr/roroph`) has 108 routes, as stated, but covers **2024–2026** operations. Names are
   GADM-style (`AgusandelNorte`) and port coordinates are partly coarse (33% of coordinates sit on a 0.1° grid). For 2018–2020 it is a proxy and needs snapping to OSM ports.
10. **NHFR blocks scripted access.** It needs a manual download, or use the fallbacks: HOT OSM health facilities and the UP NOAH
    critical-facilities layer (20,051 points, name + amenity only, no facility level).
11. **IMERG V07 and the MAP friction surface 2019 are both in the GEE catalog**, so no NASA Earthdata account is needed.
12. **The Global Flood Database covers none of the four backtest storms.** In the PHL bbox, its 44 PHL-tagged events run
    2002-07-06 to 2017-06-14 (`gfd_events_phl.csv`, dates parsed from `system_index`; the `began`/`ended` properties come back
    empty). The proposal's "covers Ompong at most" is too generous: it can't validate any backtest storm.
13. **HydroRIVERS "Asia" has no Philippine rivers.** HydroSHEDS puts the Philippines in the Australasia (`au`) region:
    `HydroRIVERS_v10_au_shp.zip` gives 41,097 reaches touching PHL (4,065 outlets: many small island basins); the `as` file gives 0.
    The script and README now use `au`.
14. **CHIRPS from the IRI Data Library has a `julian_day` time axis** (2457389.0 = 2016-01-01), which xarray doesn't decode;
    convert with `T - 2440587.5` days since 1970-01-01. Land cells only (sea is NaN). 2016–2020 complete, no missing days.
15. **MAP friction encodes sea as a constant, not nodata** (3.16 min/km motorised, 60 min/km walking, ~83% of bbox cells).
    Mask it before any access computation. Land medians: 3.0 min/km motorised, 18.9 walking.
16. **IMERG GeoTIFFs from GEE carry no band names**: band *i* is day *i* of the month in the file name. The daily bbox mean
    correlates 0.79 with CHIRPS (IMERG includes sea).
17. **Sentinel-1 is plentiful around all four storms**: 223/237/260/259 IW VV scenes in [-24, +14] days over the PHL bbox
    (Ompong/Tisoy/Rolly/Ulysses; 83–100 after landfall each), so before/after pairs are available for every backtest.
18. **The development plan dates (13–24 Oct 2026) are mostly past.** As of 2026-10-22 the panel and model deadlines
    (15 and 20 Oct) were missed; replan.

## Gotchas

- The repo lives in **OneDrive**. `data/raw` is git-ignored but still synced to OneDrive (~7 GB); the venv is outside on purpose.
- In Git Bash, `python -I` ignores the user site-packages, so always use the venv interpreter
  (`~/.venvs/bantay-baha/Scripts/python`).
- pandas 3 / numpy 2 are installed (Python 3.13 venv on this machine); `.unique()` returns Arrow arrays and `np.int64(...)` reprs, so use `.tolist()` in prints.
- `earthengine authenticate` (no gcloud needed) also gives the token `sync_data.py` uses for the bucket; it sits in
  `%USERPROFILE%\.config\earthengine\`, outside the repo. EE `getDownloadURL` sometimes returns 503; `download_gee.py` retries.
- psa.gov.ph and nhfr.doh.gov.ph return 403 to scripts. The CHIRPS IRI server drops long transfers; just re-run.
- Copernicus EMS vector zips sit on a static S3 bucket. The script scrapes the activation page for the links.
- A full forecast stage takes ~12 min (8 refits x PCMCI+ ~50 s + 10 quantile fits); the 23-quantile XGBoost is the
  slow part, so keep `n_estimators` modest. PCMCI+ with pc_alpha 0.05 ran for >10 min without finishing.
- `build.py` prints every missing input at the end; `status.json` keeps the last result per input key.
